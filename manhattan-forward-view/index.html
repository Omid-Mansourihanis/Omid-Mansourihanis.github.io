Manhattan Forward View

How much of the street ahead can a driver see? Click any street.

← Omid Mansourihanis

Click a street on the map. Building footprints load from OpenStreetMap on the first click in an area.

Each click finds the street segment between two intersections, places five points between 30% and 70% of its length and casts 73 rays, 2.5° apart, across the half plane ahead of the driver. The forward opening θ(R) is the angle covered by rays that travel beyond the look-ahead distance R before meeting a building.

    What the study found, and what this page does not claim

    In the paper's pre-specified model, each standard deviation of opening at 80 ft was associated with a lower rate of mid-block injury crashes (IRR 0.805, 95% CI 0.729 to 0.890), and wider carriageways with a higher rate (IRR 1.356), across 4,373 crashes on 4,902 segments from 2021 to 2025. No traffic volumes or speeds were observed, so these are associations, not effects, and this page does not turn them into a risk score for any street.

    Values here are computed live from OpenStreetMap footprints and streets. The paper used NYC planimetric footprints and the city's speed-limit layer, so a street's value here can differ from the paper's. The geometry is planar: building height, trees and parked cars do not block rays.

    Method: Mansourihanis, O. (2026). How much of the street ahead can a driver see? Forward views from open building footprints in Manhattan and their association with injury crashes. Manuscript, not yet peer reviewed.
