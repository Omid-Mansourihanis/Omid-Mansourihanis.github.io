What is this?

In 2019, while I was the urban-planning expert and head of the spatial database at Sari Municipality, I designed, compiled and proposed a rule table for the city's new detailed plan. For every zone and sub-zone it sets how much of a lot may be built on, how many floors, the floor-area ratio and the setbacks, and these change with the lot's area, the width of the street in front of it and which side of the street it is on.

This tool turns that table into a schematic 3D model. Choose your lot in five steps and the panel shows the rules that apply, while the 3D view shows the building they allow. The numbers come straight from the table (Alternative A); they are a proposal, not adopted rules, and they do not replace a permit enquiry at the municipality.

1 Zone

The detailed plan divides the city into land-use zones. R zones are residential; M zones mix housing with shops, offices and services.

2 Sub-zone

R1, R2 and R3 are low, medium and high residential density. In mixed codes such as M3R2, the first part is the activity level (M1 neighbourhood, M2 district, M3 regional and M4 city services, with housing; M5 city activity without housing) and the second is the density class (R1–R3), which sets the building rules. M6 is the high-rise mixed zone.

3 Lot-size class

Rules step with the lot area. These classes are the lot-size brackets of the table (the minimum-subdivision column). Larger lots usually get more floors but a lower coverage, so the building is taller and leaves more open ground. You can set the exact area in step 6.

4 Street width

The width of the street the lot faces. Wider streets allow more floors and a higher FAR. The table has no rules for streets narrower than 8 m or between 8 and 10 m (for example 9 m), so those widths are not offered.

5 Lot position relative to the street

Click the side of your lot where the street is.

In Iranian practice a northern lot lies on the north side of its street, so the street runs along its southern edge. Setbacks differ by side: the street side and the rear get their own distances, and in M zones there is no setback from the street side. By default the tool places the building against the north edge of the lot (northern placement, common in Iran), so the yard faces south.

6 Exact dimensions (optional)

The rules depend only on the class you picked, but the drawing and the floor-area figures use the exact area. The lot is drawn as a rectangle, twice as deep as it is wide unless you change the frontage.

How to read the rules
Lot coverage
The share of the lot area the building's footprint may cover. 60% on a 200 m² lot means a footprint of up to 120 m²; the rest stays open.
Floors
The maximum number of floors. Half floors (such as 2.5) come from the table, where floors = FAR ÷ coverage; the half is drawn as a lighter part of the top level.
FAR (floor-area ratio, تراکم ساختمانی)
Total floor area allowed, as a share of the lot area. 150% on a 200 m² lot allows 300 m² of floor space. In the table, FAR = floors × coverage; the tool uses that exact product (for example 316.25% where the table prints 316%).
Setback
The minimum distance between the building and a lot edge, shown as red strips. "None" means the building may reach that edge.
Buildable envelope
The blue box: the volume left after the setbacks, up to the allowed height. The building must fit inside it.
Minimum subdivision (حداقل نصاب تفکیک)
The smallest lot that may be created when land is newly divided. Smaller lots from earlier subdivisions still exist, and the table gives them rules through its lot-size classes.
Podium and tower (M6)
In the high-rise zone the table gives two parts: a podium of up to 3 floors with 60% coverage, and a main building (tower) of 12 or 14 floors with 35% coverage and larger setbacks.
Change from the previous street class
How much FAR, floors and coverage rise when the same lot faces the next wider street class, matching the difference columns of the table.
Assumptions in the drawing
  • The lot is a rectangle; by default its depth is twice its frontage.
  • Each floor is 3.2 m high (adjustable), so heights are approximate.
  • The building fills the full width between the side setbacks and is placed against the north edge (eastern and western lots: against the rear), unless you change it in step 6.
  • If the setbacks leave less room than the allowed coverage, the tool says so and draws what fits.
  • In M6 the tower is drawn on top of the podium, with its own setbacks.

Source: Proposed rules for land use and construction in zones, sub-zones and lots, detailed plan of Sari, Alternative A (2019), designed, compiled and proposed by Omid Mansourihanis, urban-planning expert and head of the spatial database, Sari Municipality.
